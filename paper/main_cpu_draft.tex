\section{Introduction}
Tiny Recursive Models (TRMs) obtain substantial reasoning capability by
repeatedly refining a compact latent state rather than scaling parameter count
\cite{trm}. This compression raises a natural question: is the current
recursive latent always a sufficient summary of the reasoning trajectory, or
can explicit access to previous latent states improve the recursive process?
We study latent-history retrieval in TRM without assuming that such memory must
be beneficial. Our primary method introduces low-rank temporal attention over
the causal sequence of within-cycle latent states, while preserving the
original spatial attention mechanism.

Our contribution is threefold. First, a supporting outer-state study shows
that explicit latent-history retrieval can help in a specific recursive
regime, with a reproducible gain at intermediate recursion depth. Second, we
introduce a proposal-faithful within-cycle Low-Rank HistoryAttention mechanism
and two matched controls that separate history access, learned selectivity, and
additional capacity. Third, a five-seed reduced-compute study shows that the
within-cycle mechanism changes optimization dynamics but does not yield a
robust final accuracy gain at small scale. We therefore evaluate whether this
behavior transfers to a larger GPU-trained regime.

\section{Related Work}
TRM simplifies recursive reasoning into repeated refinement with a small
shared network \cite{trm}, building on the broader line of hierarchical and
recurrent reasoning models such as HRM \cite{hrm}. Our work is related to
recurrent memory and temporal attention, but differs from standard
sequence-position attention: retrieval is performed across \emph{recursive
time} for the same Sudoku cell/token position. The method therefore augments,

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Within-cycle latent history.}
For each H-cycle, we initialize the temporal history with the entering latent
$z_L^0$. At L-step $t$, the current latent reads only from
$\{z_L^0,\ldots,z_L^{t-1}\}$, the TRM block produces $z_L^t$, and the new state
is appended afterwards. The history is reset at every H-cycle boundary and is
not carried through ACT.

\paragraph{Low-Rank HistoryAttention.}
For current latent $z$ and historical states $h_i$, we compute
\begin{align}
q &= W_Q\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(z), &
k_i &= W_K\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h_i),\\
v_i &= W_V\,\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h_i), &
\alpha_i &= \mathrm{softmax}_i(q^\top k_i/\sqrt{d_h}),
\end{align}
followed by
\begin{equation}
m=W_O\sum_i\alpha_i v_i,\qquad
z_{\mathrm{read}}=\mathrm{RMSNorm}\!\left(z+\sigma(g)m\right).
\end{equation}
The Q/K/V/O maps are low rank with $r/D=1/4$. The scalar gate is initialized
with $g=-2$.

\paragraph{Controls.}
We compare four matched variants: Vanilla TRM; Gated Uniform History, which
receives the same causal history but averages it without learned temporal
selection; Low-Rank HistoryAttention; and Parameter-Matched No-History, which
receives no historical states but adds an equally sized low-rank current-state
transform. These comparisons isolate history access, selective retrieval, and
capacity.

\section{Results and Contributions}
\paragraph{Supporting outer-history study.}
In a reduced CPU study over outer recursive states, the strongest
HistoryAttention effect occurred at depth 8: token accuracy increased from
$42.659\%$ to $43.389\%$ ($+0.730$ pp), with a paired 95\% CI of
$[+0.367,+1.110]$ pp and positive effects in all five seeds. The effect was
small at depths 2 and 4 and disappeared again at depth 16. The depth-8 minus
depth-4 interaction CI included zero, so these results support a
regime-specific benefit rather than monotonic improvement with recursion
depth.

\paragraph{Optimization dynamics.}
The final result hides a reproducible early effect. At step 1,250,
HistoryAttention exceeded its paired Vanilla runs by $+0.688$ pp, positive in
5/5 seeds, with 95\% CI $[+0.347,+1.028]$ and a Holm-adjusted
checkpoint-level $p=0.040$. At step 2,500 the mean advantage was $+3.099$ pp
and again positive in 5/5 seeds, but it did not survive correction across the
eight checkpoints. The advantage subsequently decayed and became slightly
negative by the final checkpoint. This indicates an optimization-dynamics
effect rather than a persistent improvement.

\paragraph{Mechanistic gate evidence.}
The added branches were not inert. Starting from
$\sigma(-2)=0.119$, the final learned gates were
$0.139\pm0.011$ for HistoryAttention,
$0.228\pm0.027$ for Gated Uniform History, and
$0.136\pm0.006$ for the parameter-matched control. Gated Uniform History
therefore learned to rely substantially more on its auxiliary branch without
improving final accuracy, ruling out the simple explanation that null final
effects arise only because the added path remains closed.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
Explicit latent-history access changes TRM behavior, but its utility is
regime-dependent. A supporting outer-history experiment shows a reproducible
benefit at intermediate recursion depth, while proposal-faithful within-cycle
HistoryAttention yields a clear early optimization advantage that is not
sustained at the final CPU checkpoint. The matched controls further show that
history access, learned selection, and additional capacity do not individually
produce a robust small-scale final improvement.

The CPU experiments use a deliberately reduced configuration and achieve zero
exact-grid accuracy, so they provide token-level and mechanistic evidence
rather than improved complete Sudoku solving. The final conclusion about scale
transfer is therefore reserved for the canonical GPU experiment.
